Gray–Scott simulator / live instrument

A gradient dissipates. Watch whether anything regenerates itself and holds a boundary against it.

This is a reaction–diffusion field — two interacting concentrations relaxing an imposed gradient, the same kind of process Prigogine classified as a dissipative structure. Nothing here is scripted to look alive. Every pattern you see is the field spending its gradient the only way it can. The counter on the right is not decorative: every few frames, it re-applies your own two-condition test — components actively regenerated, boundary actively maintained — to the raw pixel field and reports how many regions currently pass.

Under the spots and mitosis presets, watch for regions that split rather than merge — the closest this field gets to self-reproduction, an autocatalytic set spending gradient by making more of itself. Under turbulent, the gradient is still dissipating just as fast, but nothing regenerates its own boundary long enough to count — structurally closer to a hurricane than a cell. Neither this simulation nor its detector empirically measures phenomenal experience. Under the paper's account, experience is not an additional property for the detector to find, but the first-person character of world-engaging activity itself. The app makes the structural criterion — the part that can be operationalized and tested here — something you can watch happen.
